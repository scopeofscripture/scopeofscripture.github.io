Men Found in Coal Deposits

Evolutionary theory predicts a strict order in the fossil record — simple organisms in the lowest rock layers, increasingly complex life forms higher up, with man appearing only in the most recent layers. A single "out of sequence" fossil, by evolutionary scientists' own admission, would falsify the theory entirely.

Ed Conrad's discovery of a fossilized human skull embedded in Carboniferous coal strata near Mahanoy City, Pennsylvania — a layer conventionally dated at over 300 million years old — presented exactly such a case. Decades of institutional resistance followed, until independent examination by a Taiwanese research team confirmed the fossil contained genuine bone cells, blood vessels, and nerve tissue matching human cranial structure.

This is not an isolated case — similar out-of-place human remains have been documented in Cretaceous, Silurian, Triassic, and Devonian strata across multiple continents, raising serious questions about the assumed timeline of both human origins and the ages assigned to the rock layers themselves.
