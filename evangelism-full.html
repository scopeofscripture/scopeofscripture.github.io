The Urgent Call to Evangelism

In a world brimming with noise, distraction, and competing narratives, the church faces a question that has echoed across centuries: why does evangelism matter? The answer is not merely pragmatic — it is deeply theological, rooted in the very heart of God and the mission He entrusted to His people.

Evangelism is not an optional activity for a select few "gifted" believers. It is the heartbeat of the Christian faith, the natural overflow of a life transformed by grace. When we truly grasp what Christ has done for us, sharing that good news becomes not a burden, but a joy.

The Mandate from Our King

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." — Matthew 28:19-20 (ESV)

These are not suggestions — they are the final marching orders of the risen Christ. The Great Commission is not a program; it is a command issued by the King of kings to every one of His followers. Jesus does not say, "Go if you feel called," or "Go if you have the right personality." He says, "Go therefore." The "therefore" points back to His authority — "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" (v. 18). We go not in our own strength, but in the power and presence of the One who conquered death.

Evangelism is an act of obedience, but it is also an act of love. We share the gospel because we have been sent by the One who first came to seek and save the lost.

The Desperate Need of the Lost

"How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone preaching? And how are they to preach unless they are sent? As it is written, 'How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the good news!'" — Romans 10:14-15 (ESV)

Paul's logic here is both simple and devastating. Salvation comes through faith in Christ. Faith comes through hearing the message of Christ. Hearing requires a messenger. The chain is unbreakable. Without evangelists, the gospel remains unheard. Without the gospel, faith cannot be born. Without faith, there is no salvation.

This is why evangelism is urgent. Every day, people around us — neighbors, coworkers, family members, strangers — are walking in darkness, separated from God, without hope in this world. They will not stumble upon the gospel by accident. Someone must tell them. Someone must cross the room, the street, or the ocean with the message of life.

The "beautiful feet" of the evangelist are not beautiful because of eloquence or charisma. They are beautiful because they carry the most important news the world has ever known.

The Power of the Spirit at Work

"But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth." — Acts 1:8 (ESV)

Jesus did not send His disciples out alone. He promised them the power of the Holy Spirit to accomplish the mission. This same Spirit dwells in every believer today. Evangelism is not a human effort; it is a Spirit-empowered witness.

Notice what Jesus calls us: witnesses. A witness does not need to be a theologian, an orator, or a scholar. A witness simply tells what they have seen and experienced. You are a witness to the grace of God in your own life. You do not need to have all the answers. You need only to speak faithfully of what Christ has done for you and what He offers to the world.

The scope is staggering — from Jerusalem (our immediate community) to the ends of the earth. No corner of the world is beyond the reach of the gospel, and no person is beyond the saving power of Christ.

A Field Growing Harder to Reach

The urgency of this mandate is sharpened further by what has happened to Christian confession across the Western nations over the last half-century. In the United States, Pew Research Center's Religious Landscape Studies show the share of American adults identifying as Christian falling from roughly 90% in the early 1970s to about 62% by 2023-24 — a decline that, while recently showing signs of levelling off, still represents a profound generational shift away from the faith. In the United Kingdom, the change has been even starker: the 2021 Census recorded that only 46.2% of people in England and Wales described themselves as Christian, down from 59.3% a decade earlier and from 72% in 2001 — the first time in over a thousand years that Christians have not formed a majority of the population, with those reporting "no religion" rising to 37.2%. In Scotland, the 2022 census found the non-religious outright outnumbering the religious for the first time in that nation's recorded history. Across Northwestern Europe more broadly, Pew Research's demographic projections have identified the religiously unaffiliated as on track to become the largest single group in countries such as France and the Netherlands. What was, within living memory, the presumed confession of entire nations across the former British Commonwealth and its neighbours, has in the space of a few decades become a minority position in many of them.

This is precisely the field into which the Great Commission still sends the church. The harvest has not grown smaller — it has grown less familiar with the One who owns it.

Gospel evangelism is the lifeblood of the church and the hope of the world. It is rooted in the command of Christ, driven by the desperate need of the lost, and empowered by the Holy Spirit. To neglect evangelism is to silence the very message that has the power to raise the dead to life.

The question is not whether we should evangelize. The question is: will we obey? Will we open our mouths, cross our comfort zones, and speak the words of life to a dying world?

The fields are white for harvest. The King has spoken. The Spirit has been given. The need is great. The time is now.

The gospel is still the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes. Jesus is the same yesterday, today, and forever, and what is recorded in the Gospel accounts and the Book of Acts is still real for the believer. And as it says in Daniel 11:32 (KJV), "And such as do wickedly against the covenant shall he corrupt by flatteries: but the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits." So for a concise, scripturally defined example of this, get a copy of Evangelism — it's a must for every sincere Christian desiring to serve the Lord.
