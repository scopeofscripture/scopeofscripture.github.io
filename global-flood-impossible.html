A Global Flood — Impossible? Not Enough Water?

A common objection to a worldwide Flood is that there simply isn't enough water on earth to cover the mountains. But consider: about 71 percent of the globe is already covered by water, and the ocean floor is on average 5½ times deeper than the land is high — meaning roughly 18 times more water exists below sea level than dry land sits above it.

If the earth's surface were smoothed out entirely — no mountains, no ocean trenches — the water alone would cover it to a depth of roughly 1½ miles. Pre-Flood mountains, geological evidence suggests, were considerably lower than today's ranges.

Three sources are proposed for the Flood waters: vast underground basins broken open at the Deluge, an estimated 30,000 volcanoes erupting in unison, and a water canopy enveloping the earth that collapsed as continuous rain for six weeks. Remarkably, a significant portion of today's ocean volume is understood to be Floodwater that never fully receded.
