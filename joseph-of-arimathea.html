Joseph of Arimathea — Beyond Jerusalem!

Following the crucifixion of Jesus in 31 AD, Joseph of Arimathea — a wealthy member of the Jewish Council and, according to Jewish Talmudic sources, a great-uncle of Jesus — donated his own tomb for Jesus's burial, becoming a marked man among the authorities.

Cast adrift at sea around 35 AD along with eleven companions, Joseph's group survived and eventually settled in Glastonbury, Britain, carrying with them early eyewitness written records of Jesus's life and ministry.

Remarkably, independent archaeological teams working the Jerusalem crucifixion site and researchers tracing the Glastonbury group's surviving papers converged decades later at the same remote location on the opposite side of the world — where a personal copy of Joseph's own eyewitness account was recovered.
