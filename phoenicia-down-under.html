Archaeological Discoveries

Phoenicia — Biblical Tyre, Sidon and Israel, Down Under

In 2000, amateur historian Val Osborn traced evidence of Phoenician mining activity dating to around 1000 BC on the coast of Queensland, Australia — including massive engineered sea walls, a Canaanite altar, and a Baal temple.

The find potentially corroborates evidence in other research pointing to Phoenician and Egyptian voyages to Australia, possibly connected to the Red Sea fleet of Solomon and Hiram of Tyre described in Scripture.

Despite the significance of the discovery, academic and media interest was swiftly shut down, and key artifacts have since gone missing — a pattern of suppression explored in full in the companion eBook.
