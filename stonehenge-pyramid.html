A Changed Order of Things — Stonehenge and the Great Pyramid

Ancient calendars worldwide — Chinese, Babylonian, Roman, Mayan, Indian, and Egyptian alike — all originally measured a 360-day year, later independently revised to 365¼ days. This universal, simultaneous recalibration has long puzzled scholars.

The Great Pyramid's dimensions precisely encode the post-Flood value of 365.2422 days per year, while still being built during the era of the 360-day calendar — suggesting its builders possessed advanced post-Flood survey data. Stonehenge, meanwhile, functioned as a highly precise astronomical computer, its 56 Aubrey holes matching the moon's 18.61-year declination cycle down to exact whole numbers.

The most coherent explanation for these otherwise "changed" astronomical constants is a sudden shift in the earth's orbit and axial tilt at the time of a global catastrophe — consistent with the biblical Flood.
