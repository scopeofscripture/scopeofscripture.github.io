Etymology

No evidence for evolution of language

Can't be! Surely Babel is a myth. Evolutionary theory holds that human languages developed from simple, meaningless sounds toward ever more complex ones. But the most highly structured and grammatically complex languages are often found among the most "primitive" tribes — and no society at any point in history has been found with anything less than a fully developed language. There is no primitive language, and no evidence of language evolving from simple to complex.

Instead, traditions across the ancient world point the other way — to an original single language, later confused. A Sumerian tablet speaks of a time when "the whole universe" spoke "in one tongue." Ancient Egyptian, Indian, and Mesoamerican writings carry the same memory. In 2002, petroglyphs found in Colorado turned out to be nearly identical to ancient alphabetic symbols found independently in Israel, Australia, Yemen, Chile, and the British Isles — the same script on six continents.

Genesis records the moment this changed: at Babel, a rebellion under Nimrod built a tower "whose top may reach unto heaven," and in response, language was suddenly confused, scattering the builders across the earth. Remarkably, this same event appears — independently — in Toltec, Chaldean, Hindu, Fijian, Australian Aboriginal, and other traditions worldwide, each describing a great tower, a confusion of tongues, and a subsequent dispersion.
