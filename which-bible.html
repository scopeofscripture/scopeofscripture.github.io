Which Bible? And Does It Really Matter?

Since 1881, doubt has been cast on roughly 10 percent of the New Testament text across more than 5,000 locations, effectively producing two distinct manuscript traditions behind modern Bible translations.

The Antiochian tradition — with over 5,600 supporting manuscripts, tracing directly to the early church at Antioch — stands behind the King James and other Reformation-era Protestant Bibles. The Alexandrian tradition, by contrast, rests on a mere 45 manuscripts that rarely agree with each other, and has a documented history connecting it to Roman Catholic revision.

The choice between these two textual streams carries real consequences for how Scripture is read and understood — a subject explored fully in the companion volume.
