Wolves in Sheep's Clothing

BE YE THEREFORE AS WISE AS SERPENTS AND HARMLESS AS DOVES

When considering the Jesuits we should not ignore the admonition of scripture

Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Mat 7:15

Armed with this as sound advice lets continue our quest to discover more about the Jesuits. Rome commemorates the death of Ignatius of Loyola on July 31 each year. Loyola was the notorious founder of the Society of Jesus, also known as the "Jesuits". While the man died long ago, his movement lives on continuing its malicious mission across the globe. This Jesuit agenda has relevance today for America, Europe, and many other nations around the world.

Over the years many scholars and historians have written about the nefarious schemes of the Jesuits and Former Catholic priest Richard Bennett also recognized the danger:

It is a well-established fact that the Jesuits throughout their history have caused many serious disturbances by their nefarious schemes within the civil governments of many countries. Over the centuries, they have justifiably earned their reputation as troublemakers to the extent that they were denied residence in some nations for varying periods of time.

The historian J.A. Wylie draws remarkable parallels between Loyola and the Jesuits to Luther and the Reformers. His comparison goes beyond the fact that both men were born and lived around the same time in the 1500's. Both men began as stubborn, secular careerists. Near disastrous events turned their lives to strict, even fanatical, Catholicism.

Loyola was a military man who suffered a near fatal injury. Luther was set on a career as a lawyer until nearly being struck dead by lightning. Both men turned to extreme asceticism in Catholic monasteries. Both Loyola and Luther were tortured by their consciences as the guilt of their sin weighed heavily upon them. This austere Catholicism brought out their passion, genius, and organizational skills.

Yet, this is where the similarities end.

Luther's despair directed him toward repentance toward God and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ, whereby he came to an understanding of faith in Christ alone through grace alone. Following this Luther began to teach others and as he continued, he and other reformers rediscovered the core doctrines of the Christian faith that are contained in Scripture. God's working in Luther's life led to the liberation of many from dead works of the false doctrines of Rome. It was once commonly understood that the reformers discovered 2 very important truths, they discovered the real Christ of Scripture and His counterpart and adversary the Anti Christ of Scripture.

In contrast, Loyola's mind dug deeper into the well of Roman mysticism and Babylonian substitution. He performed acts of self-humiliation and masochism to further his devotion to "Mary". (Semiramis) This asceticism made Loyola claim he saw fantastic visions of Heaven and Hell, Mary and Jesus. Eventually, he convinced himself to become a "knight for Mary" serving as a "soldier of Jesus". He would offer his unconditional loyalty to the Roman Pope and actively recruited other men to his fanatic cause of countering opposition to Rome, as he aggressively targeted reformed Christians.

Loyola named his group "the Society of Jesus" and prior to this order other orders such as the Augustinians, Dominicans, and Franciscans conducted similar anti-Christian activities, like persecuting the Albigenses and Waldenses. So, he was in good company and as his order developed it would take on the main role of countering Christians as the reformation developed.

Over time the Jesuits would develop into a religious and political order that would as a cloak, serve the least fortunate in society, such as the sick and poor, they would open schools and colleges but ultimately their primary goal was to influence the areas where power emanated from, in both the catholic and protestant countries. This would be achieved by controlling the ruling classes, to subject the population to the Roman Pope and erode the dissenting belief in the Bible alone, as the Protestants had established through the reformation. Hence, many refer to the Jesuits activities as the "Counter Reformation"
